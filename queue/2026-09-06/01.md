💰 Why is it almost impossible for a Premier League giant to go bankrupt from bad transfers anymore?

💸 The Premier League’s financial landscape has evolved to a point where traditional economic rules no longer apply. While Manchester City topped the charts spending £457.5m and Crystal Palace sat at the bottom on £50m, the real story is how clubs survive their own worst mistakes.

📈 Chelsea’s early Clearlake era saw a chaotic £200m spent in eight months, leaving just Malo Gusto and Wesley Fofana behind. Yet, instead of financial ruin, the market's natural inflation means underperforming players are still flipped as growth assets, completely shielding clubs from bad recruitment.

📊 It turns out that simply owning a spot in the division acts as a central bank. With Liverpool recording a high net spend of £216.8m and Aston Villa managing a £56.7m net gain while rebuilding, the entire league operates in a financial stratosphere where asset values keep climbing. 

Will this economic bubble ever burst, or is English football simply too big to fail?
