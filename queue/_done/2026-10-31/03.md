Liverpool made headlines with heavy spending, yet left obvious squad gaps unfilled. Here is why Iraola defended the strategy.

🧠 Despite recording the Premier League high net outlay, Liverpool opted against buying a dedicated right-back, defensive midfielder, or natural right-winger. Iraola defended the approach, warning that throwing huge money at temporary fixes restricts financial muscle for future transfer windows.

🩹 Rather than panic buying for a two-month fix while players like Hugo Ekitiké, Conor Bradley, and Giovanni Leoni recover from injuries, Liverpool trust internal squad depth. The coaching staff believes adaptable attackers like Bradley Barcola can effectively cover right-sided roles without compromising quality.

📈 By prioritizing elite profile targets over positional placeholders, the club aims to preserve long-term project flexibility while trusting existing players to step up across multiple positions.
