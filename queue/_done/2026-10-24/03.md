English football's new regulator will cost clubs £20.3m per year, double the original £10m forecast.

💰 The annual operating budget includes £18.3m for 100 staff members, £1m reserved for potential legal disputes, and £1m towards an estimated £45m setup cost spread over ten years. Every club across the top five divisions will be required to pay into the scheme starting next season.

📊 Payments are structured around revenue and competition tier. European participants pay up to £1.8m annually, standard Championship clubs without parachute payments pay £45,000, while League One and Two sides pay £9,000 and £6,000 respectively. The watchdog highlighted that 94% of English clubs are currently loss-making, justifying the need for oversight.

🏛️ Opposition politicians have questioned the ballooning costs, arguing the body is far from the light-touch regulator originally promised. The regulator maintains that the levy accounts for roughly 0.1% of revenue for most clubs and is far lower than existing Premier League administrative expenses.
