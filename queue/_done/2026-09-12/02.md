How do you spend £1bn and still end up with the exact same starting defense from two years ago?

📊 Despite being the Premier League's highest net spenders since 2022, Manchester United enter the Champions League with severe squad limitations. Last season's light 40-game schedule protected them, but a potential 67-game campaign this year will expose their lack of rotation options.

💸 The issue lies in poor allocation. Only 19% of their billion-pound outlay went toward defenders, forcing them to field the same backline from Erik ten Hag's first game in 2022. Up front, heavy spending on supporting attackers leaves them with only one recognized striker.

🔄 With a retention rate of over 54% of their 2022 starting core, United have prioritized continuity over necessary evolution, leaving their squad values lagging behind rivals in midfield and defense.
