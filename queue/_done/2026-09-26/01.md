🔵 Pedro Neto’s new five-year contract proves Chelsea’s long-term wage strategy actually works.

The Portuguese winger rejected heavy summer interest from Manchester City to stay at Stamford Bridge under Xabi Alonso. Chelsea have rewarded this loyalty and his strong start—two goals in five games as an attacking wingback—with an improved contract and an immediate pay rise.

This rapid adjustment demonstrates Chelsea's willingness to upgrade long-term deals for top performers. It stands in stark contrast to Enzo Fernández, who forced a £125m move to Manchester City after falling out with the board and raising questions about his future.

By rewarding Neto, the hierarchy is sending a clear message: players who buy into the project get rewarded, while those who disrupt the squad will be sold. Do you think this heavily incentivized contract system is the future of football?
