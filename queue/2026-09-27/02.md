Promoted Premier League clubs are no longer playing it safe, splashing up to £194m each just to survive.

📈 The financial landscape of the Premier League has reached a tipping point. Newly promoted Ipswich Town led the charge by spending £194.7m on 15 players, while Hull City signed 18 players for £156.5m. Even Coventry City splashed £122.4m on 11 new signings to rebuild their squad for the top flight.

🔄 This is a massive shift from 2021, when the three promoted sides spent a combined £98.8m. Meanwhile, other leagues are functioning as feeder systems. In Ligue 1, 15 out of 18 clubs recorded a net profit, led by Paris Saint-Germain's £150m surplus, heavily boosted by Bradley Barcola’s £123m move to Liverpool.

🌍 The summer window also saw clubs like Real Madrid and Tottenham acting early before the World Cup. Real Madrid secured Marc Cucurella for £52m and Bernardo Silva early on, later adding breakout star Yan Diomande for £115.7m, showing that elite clubs will pay premium prices to secure proven talent.
