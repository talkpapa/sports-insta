🔄 Chelsea are drawing a clear line in the sand: loyalty and performance get rewarded, while disruption gets sold.

Pedro Neto has signed an improved five-year deal with a pay rise after rejecting summer interest from Manchester City to play under Xabi Alonso. Neto's commitment contrasts sharply with Enzo Fernández, who forced a £125m move to City on deadline day after submitting a written transfer request.

Chelsea's hierarchy chose to sell Fernández after concluding his relationship with the squad was broken following public criticisms. By rewarding Neto alongside other core players like João Pedro and Moisés Caicedo, the club is proving that their long-term contract model will actively reward players who buy into the project.
