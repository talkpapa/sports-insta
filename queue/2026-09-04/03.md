🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 Premier League clubs have signed 100 teenagers in five years. Here is how they are monopolising the global market.

📈 English clubs are vacuuming up prospects at unprecedented rates. Newcastle paid £20m for 18-year-old Sean Steur after just 20 senior games, while Manchester City spent £81.5m on teenager Ayyoub Bouaddi. By comparison, La Liga clubs average just 5.8 teenage signings per year compared to the Premier League's 20.

💰 This aggressive scouting creates a self-contained ecosystem. Because English teams buy talent so early, the best players mature inside England. When they want to move, only other Premier League clubs possess the financial power to pay their massive transfer fees and wages.

🔄 Financial regulations like squad cost ratios also encourage high-volume domestic swaps. This summer, a massive portion of Premier League clubs made their most expensive signings from direct domestic rivals, keeping the wealth and talent circulating within one country.

Will this aggressive hoarding eventually kill the transfer market for the rest of Europe?
