🚨 The Premier League isn't just buying established stars anymore—it is actively monopolizing the world's best teenage talent.

Over the last five seasons, English top-flight clubs completed 100 permanent transfers for teenagers, averaging 20 per year. Compare that to Spain's La Liga, which signed just 29 teenagers in the same span. Massive deals like Manchester City paying $110m for 18-year-old Ayyoub Bouaddi and Aston Villa spending $63m on Ibrahim Mbaye show the sheer scale of this financial aggression.

But this youth hoarding has a secondary effect. Because the best young players are vacuumed into England so early, they mature inside the country. When they are ready for major moves, only other wealthy Premier League clubs can afford them. This, combined with squad cost ratio rules that favor player swaps, has created an incredibly insular and self-sustaining domestic market.
